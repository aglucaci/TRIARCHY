TRIARCHY recovered four distinct environmental microbial communities while identifying several reasons why individual sequencing assays disagreed. Return-air samples contained increased host DNA and human-associated microbial signatures, outdoor air was enriched for fungal signals, supply air displayed reduced community complexity, and settled dust contained the greatest mixed-community diversity. Control-aware integration identified reagent-, batch-, and assay-specific contaminants while preserving authentic biological signals. Cross-assay interpretation distinguished concordant observations from primer-limited, rank-limited, and reference-limited detections rather than treating all assay disagreement as biological discordance.
